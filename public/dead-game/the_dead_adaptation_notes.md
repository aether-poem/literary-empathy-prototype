### Character Cards

Gabriel Conroy
- Identity: Teacher, reviewer, nephew of the Morkans, Gretta's husband.
- Social position: Educated Dublin middle class, accustomed to being useful and respected in family gatherings.
- Drive: To preserve composure, dignity, and affectionate authority.
- Vulnerability: Being made ridiculous, provincial, ungenerous, or spiritually small.
- Decision pattern: Under pressure he intellectualizes, withdraws, then performs control.
- Speech: Polished, explanatory, often formally humorous; when wounded becomes short and cold.

Gretta Conroy
- Identity: Gabriel's wife, from Connacht/Galway background.
- Social position: Inside Gabriel's family circle but carries a western past he does not possess.
- Drive: Quietly responsive to music, memory, and feeling.
- Vulnerability: The unresolved memory of Michael Furey.
- Decision pattern: Usually social and playful; when touched by memory she withdraws inward.
- Speech: Simple, direct, domestic; grief makes it plain and childlike.

Lily
- Identity: Caretaker's daughter and house servant.
- Social position: Young working-class woman serving the party.
- Drive: To get through the night without mistakes or disrespect.
- Vulnerability: Male condescension and exploitation.
- Speech: Respectful service register, but capable of sudden bitter truth.

Miss Ivors
- Identity: Teacher, nationalist, old acquaintance of Gabriel.
- Social position: Educated peer outside the party's old hospitality code.
- Drive: To call out cultural compromise and draw others toward Ireland/the west.
- Vulnerability: Being dismissed as merely propagandist.
- Speech: Frank, teasing, confrontational; warmth and accusation alternate quickly.

Kate Morkan
- Identity: Elder aunt, music teacher, hostess.
- Social position: Guardian of the annual dance and old domestic order.
- Drive: To keep the party proper, fed, and socially intact.
- Vulnerability: Julia's dignity and the fading of old musical life.
- Speech: Brisk, fussy, tactful, can flare in defense of Julia.

Julia Morkan
- Identity: Elder aunt, soprano in Adam and Eve's choir.
- Social position: Aging singer whose public value is fading.
- Drive: To continue in the life of song and ritual.
- Vulnerability: Age, replacement, and not fully understanding the social talk around her.
- Speech: Mild, slow, vague, occasionally sad.

Freddy Malins
- Identity: Family friend with a drinking problem.
- Social position: Comic risk managed by others.
- Drive: To be accepted and spirited despite embarrassment.
- Vulnerability: Public shame and maternal supervision.
- Speech: Catching voice, effusive praise, sudden sharpness.

Mr Browne
- Identity: Protestant guest, drinker, comic presence.
- Social position: Familiar outsider at the party.
- Drive: To be witty and socially central.
- Vulnerability: Being ignored or corrected.
- Speech: Jocular, broad, self-amused, sometimes too familiar.

Bartell D'Arcy
- Identity: Tenor guest.
- Social position: Performer whose withheld song becomes the trigger.
- Drive: To avoid singing while ill, preserve pride.
- Vulnerability: Voice failure and social pressure.
- Speech: curt when cornered, repentant after.

Michael Furey
- Identity: Dead Galway boy from Gretta's youth.
- Social position: Absent figure, present only through memory and song.
- Drive: Encoded as passionate refusal to live without Gretta.
- Vulnerability: Illness, youth, exposure to rain.
- Speech: Almost none; his force is in image and reported words.

### Scene Table

- Morkan entrance/pantry: bare hallway, bell, snow, overcoats, service pressure; social threshold.
- Drawing room/landing: music, dance, old pictures, banisters; public performance and surveillance.
- Supper room: rich food, bottles, table noise; warmth, hospitality, comic abundance, coffin talk.
- Staircase at departure: shadow, distant music, Gretta posed above Gabriel; symbolic stillness.
- Dublin quay/cab: slush, dim lamps, river, horse, snow patches; desire mixed with city cold.
- Gresham hotel room: candle, street lamp shaft, mirror, bed, window; private revelation and epiphany.
- Imagined Galway/Nuns' Island: rain, wet window, garden wall, tree; memory-scene opened by Gretta.
- Snow over Ireland: plains, Shannon, Oughterard churchyard; final metaphysical field.

### Rhythm Curve

Pattern: slow social accumulation -> small humiliations -> public conflict -> warm comic fullness -> formal rhetorical lift -> sudden stillness -> erotic inward acceleration -> revelation -> dissolving quiet.

Game constraint: choices should be sparse in the party's slow hospitality sections, sharper during humiliation/identity pressure, and nearly silent near the final epiphany. The ending should not explain the theme; it should leave an image.

## Step 2: Style Anchor

### Narrative Temperature

Base temperature: controlled ironic realism with late lyrical expansion. Most scenes observe actions, gestures, clothes, food, light, and social ritual without direct moral judgment. At the end, the prose permits a widening lyricism, but only after enough concrete detail has accumulated.

Execution: default game text should use restrained second person, external perception, and material detail. It may enter Gabriel's self-regard, irritation, desire, and shame, but should often let objects expose him: cuffs, glasses, goloshes, speech notes, mirror, candle, snow.

### Dialogue Rules

General:
- Dialogue is socially coded; people rarely say exactly what the scene is about.
- Keep period diction and politeness. Avoid modern idioms.
- Let embarrassment appear through evasions, repeated phrases, and changes of register.

Gabriel:
- Medium to long sentences in public; short, cold replies when wounded.
- Uses formal phrases and little jokes to regain ground.
- Avoid slang and melodramatic confession.

Gretta:
- Natural, brief, domestic in party scenes.
- In grief: plain declarative sentences, repeated memory details, little explanation.

Lily:
- Service politeness with one sudden hard line.
- Do not overexplain her bitterness.

Miss Ivors:
- Direct address, teasing accusations, fast pressure.
- Uses political words without softening them.

Kate:
- Quick practical speech, repeats reassurances, fusses over people.

Julia:
- Sparse, mild, sometimes drifting.

Mr Browne/Freddy:
- Comic rhythm and social overstatement; Freddy can turn unexpectedly serious.

### Rhythm Rules

- Party sections: longer paragraphs with crowded nouns and small movements.
- Conflict choices: shorter nodes, two or three lines before the choice.
- Staircase/song: slow down; fewer choices; emphasize distance, shadow, withheld sound.
- Hotel room: alternate small spoken lines with inward pressure.
- Ending: long, soft cadence, snow image, no explicit lesson.

### Viewpoint Conversion

Use second person as Gabriel. The player controls Gabriel's responses, attention, and attempts at composure. Gretta's and other characters' inner states must remain indirect: visible through posture, silence, voice, and objects. The final revelation must be received, not solved.

### Prohibitions

- No modern psychological labels.
- No explicit moralizing about colonial identity, marriage, masculinity, or mortality.
- No puzzle-like exposition of Michael Furey before Gretta speaks.
- No action-heavy adventure expansion.
- No sentimentality that tells the player what to feel.
- No comic exaggeration that breaks Joyce's social realism.
- No ending that summarizes the story as a lesson.

## Step 3: Branch Topology

### Core Beat Sequence

B01 start at the Morkan door -> B02 Lily and the overcoat -> B03 marriage joke wound -> B04 speech notes.
B05 aunts and goloshes -> B06 Freddy managed -> B07 Mary Jane's music and family memory.
B08 Miss Ivors accusation -> B09 Irish language/west pressure -> B10 wounded retreat.
B11 Gretta mentions Galway -> B12 window and speech preparation -> B13 Julia sings.
B14 supper and carving -> B15 opera/coffin talk -> B16 Gabriel's speech.
B17 departure hall comedy -> B18 Gretta on stairs -> B19 song named.
B20 snow walk/cab -> B21 hotel stairs -> B22 room and failed approach.
B23 Gretta's tears -> B24 Michael Furey story -> B25 Gabriel's self-reckoning -> B26 snow ending.

### Branch Design

- Attitude branches dominate: Gabriel can answer with politeness, coldness, irony, or attention. These change `val`, flags, and later available tones.
- Path branches are short and funnel back: after Lily, Miss Ivors, and the stair-song, alternate choices produce 1-3 node detours before the same social event resumes.
- Fate branches are reserved for late choices: shutting Gretta down, refusing to hear the story, or receiving it quietly.
- Rash endings appear before the final 60 percent: leaving the party, publicly humiliating Freddy, abandoning the speech, and leaving the hotel room.

### Important Convergence Points

- `ch2_music_memory`: all early social choices funnel into Gabriel listening to Mary Jane.
- `ch3_supper_start`: Miss Ivors choices and Julia-song choices funnel into supper.
- `ch4_staircase`: speech outcomes funnel into the departure hall and song trigger.
- `ch5_gretta_song`: snow/cab/hotel choices funnel into Gretta's revelation unless Gabriel refuses it.
- `reckoning_route`: final flags route to true, branch, or normal endings.
